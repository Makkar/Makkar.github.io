Statistical Optimal Transport

Suppose XX and YY are ℝd−\mathbb R^d-valued random vectors on a probability space (Ω,ℱ,ℙ)(\Omega, \mathscr F, \mathbb P) with distributions μ:=ℙ∘X−1\mu := \mathbb P \circ X^{-1} and ν:=ℙ∘Y−1\nu := \mathbb P \circ Y^{-1} respectively. It is often of interest to find, if it exists, a measurable map T:ℝd→ℝdT \colon \mathbb R^d \to \mathbb R^d such that the pushforward T#μ:=μ∘T−1T_{\#} \mu := \mu \circ T^{-1} of μ\mu under TT equals ν.\nu. It is possible that no such map exists, for example, if μ=δx0\mu = \delta_{x_0} for some x0∈ℝdx_0 \in \mathbb R^d and ν\nu does not equal a Dirac measure, because for any measurable T:ℝd→ℝdT\colon \mathbb R^d \to \mathbb R^d the pushforward T#μT_{\#}\mu is δT(x0).\delta_{T(x_0)}.

On the other hand, if multiple such maps exist, then which one should we prefer? In the Monge's formulation of optimal transport problem, we have a measurable cost function c:ℝd×ℝd→[0,∞]c \colon \mathbb R^d \times \mathbb R^d \to [0, \infty], and we want to find an optimal map

T0∈arg minT#μ=ν⁡∫ℝdc(x,T(x))dμ(x),\begin{aligned} T_0 \in \argmin_{T_{\#}\mu = \nu} \int_{\mathbb R^d} c(x, T(x)) \, \mathrm{d}\mu(x),\end{aligned}
provided, of course, that it exists. It is not obvious when such a map exists, however, the following refinement by (McCann, 1995) of the result discovered independently by (Knott and Smith, 1984) and by (Brenier, 1987) tells us when an optimal map exists, assuming quadratic cost. As a piece of terminology, we call a set A⊆ℝdA \subseteq \mathbb R^d\;small if it has a Hausdorff dimension at most d−1.d-1. So, for example, Lebesgue-negligible sets are small.

Theorem: Let μ\mu and ν\nu be two Borel probability measures on ℝd\mathbb R^d, with dd a positive integer. Suppose that μ\mu does not give mass to small sets, and that the cost function c:ℝd×ℝd→[0,∞]c\colon \mathbb R^d \times \mathbb R^d \to [0, \infty] is the squared Euclidean norm c(x,y)=‖x−y‖2.c(x,y) = \left\lVert x-y\right\rVert^2. Then there is exactly one (upto μ−\mu-a.e. of course) measurable map T:ℝd→ℝdT \colon \mathbb R^d \to \mathbb R^d such that T#μ=νT_{\#} \mu = \nu, and moreover T=∇φT = \nabla \varphi for some convex function φ:ℝd→ℝ.\varphi \colon \mathbb R^d \to \mathbb R.

In statistical optimal transport, the explicit form of the measures μ\mu or ν\nu is unknown, and instead only independent samples from them are observed. To that end, let X1,X2,…X_1, X_2, \ldots be independent copies of X,X, and let Y1,Y2,…Y_1, Y_2, \ldots be independent copies of Y.Y. Let μn:=1n∑i=1nδXi\mu_n := \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n \delta_{X_i} denote the empirical measure based on the sample (X1,…,Xn)(X_1, \ldots, X_n) of the first nn observations, and likewise for νn.\nu_n.
